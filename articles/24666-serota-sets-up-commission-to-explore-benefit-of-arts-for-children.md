Arts Council England chair seeks proposals to ensure pupils across UK get ‘opportunities currently only available in the best schools’

Tuesday 28 March 2017 00.01 BST  Last modified on Tuesday 28 March 2017 10.31 BST

A major investigation into the best ways of nurturing creativity in young people is to be announced by the new chairman of [Arts Council England](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/arts-council-england), Sir Nicholas Serota.

Serota will use his first speech since [beginning the job in February](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2016/sep/08/sir-nicholas-serota-appointed-chairman-arts-council-england-tate) to announce the Durham commission on creativity and education.

The 18-month inquiry, a partnership between [Durham University](https://www.theguardian.com/education/durhamuniversity) and Arts Council England, will investigate what happens when children experience arts and culture and how it helps them develop and thrive.

It comes against a backdrop of concern that [creative education has been harmed by the government’s English baccalaureate, which does not make an arts subject compulsory](https://www.royalacademy.org.uk/article/why-ebacc-must-not-exclude-arts).

Serota said the commission was an opportunity to “step back, review the evidence, see what has worked and come up with some proposals” that could be considered by the arts council and the government.

The lack of mandatory arts subjects in the Ebacc has been a controversial issue for some time.

[The latest salvo was a protest letter signed by more than 100 leading cultural figures](http://www.artsprofessional.co.uk/news/arts-leaders-urge-prime-minister-drop-damaging-ebacc), which was delivered to the prime minister earlier this month. It said that in 2016, for the first time since 2012, the percentage of pupils taking at least one arts subject had declined and that teacher numbers and teaching hours in creative subjects were declining almost twice as fast as in other subjects.

Serota said it was a wider issue than just the Ebacc: “Even if they were to open the Ebacc to one arts subject, I don’t think that would be the complete solution. In my view, it would help, but it would only be a partial solution.

“What I think the Durham commission might do is come up with a number of ideas that would be applied across the country to ensure kids in all kinds of schools get the kind of opportunities that are currently available only in the best of schools.”

The commission is expected to begin work in September and report in spring 2019.

Serota, the outgoing director of the Tate after almost 30 years, is making his first speech as chair of Arts Council England in Hull on Tuesday.

It will be keenly watched for signs of changing priorities, however subtle. As well as education, Serota will say more needs to be done for communities that have never had much of an arts offer.

“In the past, sometimes the Arts Council has not supported organisations until they have got a proven track record of several years,” he said. It needed to be more prepared “to back something at a very early stage, especially when it brings the arts to communities which haven’t previously really had a chance”.

Rural communities and places on the margins of big towns and cities are particularly on his mind.

He will also talk about the intrinsic value of the arts, irrespective of what they might do for social cohesion, regeneration or tourism.

“It is literally about those magic moments when you stand in front of a painting, or listen to a piece of music, or read a book, and it gives you a new insight in to yourself or the world. We just need to hang on that.”

Serota, who replaced Sir Peter Bazalgette at Arts Council England, will formally leave the Tate on 31 May, to be succeeded by Maria Balshaw.

That will finally free up his evenings, he said, to get out more. “I won’t quite have so many receptions with Tate sponsors, as much as I love them.

“Henceforth, I shall give over most of my evenings to be involved with the arts as a whole and that is going to be fantastic for me. I have a passion for dance, I like theatre, a number different kinds of music … I’m looking forward to being able to pursue that.”
