**From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia**

**Inter Press Service** (**IPS**) is a global [news agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_agency "News agency") headquartered in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome"), Italy. Its main focus is news and analysis about social, political, civil, and economic subjects as it relates to the [Global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South "Global South"), [civil society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_society "Civil society") and [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization").

### 

### **History**

IPS was set up in 1964 as a [non-profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-profit_organization "Non-profit organization") international journalist [cooperative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative "Cooperative"). Its founders were the Italian journalist [Roberto Savio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Savio "Roberto Savio") and Argentine political scientist [Pablo Piacentini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Piacentini "Pablo Piacentini"). Initially, the primary objective was to fill the information gap between Europe and Latin America after the political turbulence following the [Cuban Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Revolution "Cuban Revolution") of 1959.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Press_Service#cite_note-history-1)[[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Press_Service#cite_note-2)[[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Press_Service#cite_note-3)

Later the network expanded to include all continents, from its Latin American base in Costa Rica in 1982.[[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Press_Service#cite_note-history-1) In 1994, IPS changed its legal status to that of a 'public-benefit organization for development cooperation'.

In 1996, IPS had permanent offices and correspondents in 41 countries, covering 108 nations. Its subscribers included over 600 print media, around 80 news agencies and database services and 65 broadcast media, in addition to over 500 NGOs and other institutions.[[4]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Press_Service#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGiffard1998-4)

### 

### **Approach**

IPS's stated aims are to present voices of [marginalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginalization "Marginalization") and vulnerable people and groups, to report from the perspectives of [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country"), and to reflect the views of [civil society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_society "Civil society"). The mainstreaming of [gender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender "Gender") in reporting and the assessment of the impacts of [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization "Globalization") are priorities.

IPS may be unique in its concentration on [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country") and the strong relationships with [civil society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_society "Civil society"). For this reason, IPS has been termed by some probably the 'largest and most credible of all 'alternatives' in the world of news agencies.'[[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Press_Service#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoyd-BarrettRantanen1998174%E2%80%93175-5) It is also considered by some as the 'first and only independent and professional news agency which provides on daily basis information with a Third World focus and point of view.'[[6]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Press_Service#cite_note-FOOTNOTEBoyd-BarrettThussu199294-6)[[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Press_Service#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGiffard1998191-7)[[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Press_Service#cite_note-FOOTNOTEFenby1986-8)
